About

I'm a backend engineer at Lentra, working on the services behind a digital lending platform. A loan application moves through intake, underwriting and disbursement, and my job is most of the plumbing in between: the Spring Boot services that carry it, and the six-plus external systems they have to talk to on the way.

A lot of that turns out to be failure handling. Bank APIs time out. Callbacks arrive twice, or never. I've spent much of the last year on retry flows that resume at the step that failed instead of starting the whole thing again, and on moving bank-specific logic out of code and into configuration, so onboarding a new bank isn't another branch in a method.

In my own time I build the same kind of system in the open. LoanIQ is a credit underwriting engine where the rule engine makes the call and the model only writes the explanation. SentinelPay scores a payment in milliseconds and leaves the slow thinking to an agent that runs after the payment is already on hold. Both ship with tests, migrations, Docker and CI, because I wanted them to be things a person could clone and actually run.

Projects

LoanIQ

Oct 2026 – Present

An AI-assisted credit underwriting engine. A regulator can ask two years later why an applicant was declined, and the answer has to come out the same every time it is asked.

Why it is built this way

A model anywhere in the decision path makes the system unauditable, because the same application can score differently on two runs. So the model reads documents and writes the rationale, and nothing else. The decision comes from a deterministic rule engine whose thresholds live in configuration.

  • Five ordered policy rules with hard stops. Every outcome is stored per application, passing rules included, so an audit shows what was considered and not only what failed.
  • Idempotency-Key is a unique column, so a client retry replays the original decision instead of underwriting a second time.
  • A missing bureau score is treated as risk rather than as neutral, which is how thin-file applications get caught instead of waved through.
  • 13 tests covering decision boundaries, determinism and idempotent replay.

SentinelPay

Oct 2026 – Present

Fraud detection for card and UPI payments, split along the line that decides the architecture: what has to be fast, and what is allowed to be slow.

Why it is built this way

Scoring sits on the payment's critical path, so it stays deterministic, model-free and fixed-cost. Investigation only runs once a payment is already held, which is exactly why it can take seconds and call a model.

  • Five risk signals scored over a single batch of context queries, so adding a signal never adds a query to the hot path.
  • Weights are additive and capped at 100, and no single signal can reach the block threshold alone. One bad signal costs precision instead of declining every customer at once.
  • An agent works held cases through an allow-list of three read-only tools. It recommends, and its recommendation is stored next to the analyst's verdict rather than replacing it, so the two can be compared later.
  • 34 tests across signals, banding boundaries, the agent loop and the full pipeline on a real database.
